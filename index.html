User Guide

Planar grid calibration & real-world measurement

What It Does

GridMeasure corrects camera tilt, rotation, skew, scale and perspective using a 4-point 2D projective transformation (homography) fitted to a grid in the photo, so you can take real-world coordinates from the image. It runs entirely in your browser; images never leave your computer.

🔬
Specimen & Lab Leaves, insects, seed sizes, or petri dish samples on millimeter grid paper.
📐
Engineering & Inspection PCB traces, component tolerances, and planar materials sizing.
🔍
Forensic & Field Work Objects photographed in situ with a planar scale marker or grid.
📊
Auditable Data Export Coordinates formatted for exact mathematical pixel reconstruction.

Loading Images

PNG, JPEG and WebP work everywhere; TIFF usually only in Safari. Files that can't be decoded are listed in a notice at the bottom of the screen. The count under Add Images shows how many images are calibrated, and each thumbnail's border shows its status:

No border: 0 points Yellow: calibrating, or calibrated with no measurements yet Green: calibrated, with measurement points Red: calibration failed — reposition P1–P4

Switch images with [ / ] (or PgUp / PgDn). Each image keeps its own points, zoom and position.

Place P1–P4

The first four clicks on an image are calibration points. Click the corners of one known grid square in counter-clockwise order:

P4 (0, 1)
P3 (1, 1)
Known Grid Square
P1 (0, 0)
P2 (1, 0)
  • P1 → (0,0): Reference origin
  • P2 → (1,0): One unit along the X-axis
  • P3 → (1,1): Opposite diagonal corner
  • P4 → (0,1): One unit along the Y-axis
Placing P4 computes the homography, shows the calibration diagnostics in the right sidebar, and draws the fitted grid over the image. Zoom in (Ctrl+scroll) or turn on the loupe (Z) to hit corners exactly.

Use a Larger Square

P1–P4 don't have to be one grid cell. Under Calibration → Grid coordinates (P1–P4) you can set any grid coordinates, e.g. (0,0), (10,0), (10,10), (0,10) for the corners of a 10×10 block. A larger square makes small clicking errors matter much less.

Set Physical Units

Enter the size of one grid unit (e.g. 10) and choose its unit (mm, cm, m, µm, in). Both are remembered in this browser and written to the CSV export.

Fixing a Calibration

  • Drag any of P1–P4, or select it and nudge with the arrow keys; calibration and all measurement points update immediately.
  • A red thumbnail border means the four points can't form a valid calibration (e.g. three in a line). Move them apart.

Place Measurement Points

After calibration, every click on an empty part of the image adds a measurement point (P5, P6, …). Its calibrated grid coordinates appear in the right sidebar and in the export; distances and other metrics are computed downstream (e.g. with the scripts in analysis/).

Adjust Points

  • Drag: press on a point and move it. A press only becomes a drag after 3 screen pixels, so clicking a point never moves it by accident.
  • Select & nudge: click a point (or its row in the sidebar) to select it — it gets a white ring. Arrow keys move it 0.1 px, Shift+arrow 1 px, Alt+arrow 10 px. Esc deselects.
  • Delete: Delete/Backspace, Delete Last, or the × on the newest point remove the last point. Earlier points can't be deleted on their own, because that would renumber the rest.
  • Reset: Reset Points or R clears all points on the image (asks first).

Zoom, Pan & Loupe

  • Zoom: Ctrl+scroll or a trackpad pinch zooms around the cursor. Fit (top-right of the canvas) or 0/F fits the image to the window; the button also shows the zoom level.
  • Pan: scroll moves vertically, Shift+scroll horizontally. You can also hold Space and drag, or drag with the middle mouse button. Panning stops at the image edges.
  • Loupe: off by default — turn it on with Z or Zoom. It magnifies the area under the cursor (2×–16×; change with +/- or Alt+scroll) with a gap crosshair and a center dot on the exact pixel.
  • Sharp pixels: from 400% zoom, image pixels are drawn as squares so you can see pixel boundaries.

Touch Screens

Tap to place a point and press-and-drag to move one. Drag an empty area to pan and pinch to zoom. The loupe appears above your finger so the spot stays visible.

Grid Overlay

On calibrated images the fitted grid is drawn back onto the photo: cyan lines at every grid unit (every 10th stronger) and the calibration square in yellow. If the lines sit on the paper's grid across the whole sheet, calibration is good. Where they drift away, measurements there are off (usually lens distortion). Press G or click Grid to hide or show it.

Calibration Diagnostics

Once P1–P4 are placed, five cards in the right sidebar describe the calibration square:

Metric What It Measures Interpretation
Rotation Angle of vector P1 → P2 relative to image horizontal Grid tilt relative to camera sensor (e.g. +2.4°). Compensated automatically.
Scale Pixel resolution per unit (px/unit) & physical density (px per unit of length) Higher values = greater precision. Compares X vs Y resolution (U × V).
Aspect Ratio Ratio of vertical to horizontal unit length (|P4-P1| / |P2-P1|) 1.000 is ideal (Square ✓). Deviations indicate non-square pixels or angled tilt.
Corner Angle Angle between P1 → P2 and P1 → P4 90.0° is ideal (Orthogonal ✓). Deviations highlight shear skew or perspective slant.
Perspective Homography projective magnitude & keystone ratio Categorized None to Strong. Confirms perspective foreshortening correction.

The cards only describe the four calibration points; the grid overlay checks the whole image.

Limitations

The correction is a single homography, exact only for a flat plane and a distortion-free lens:

  • Flat plane: the grid and object must lie on the same flat surface. Anything raised above the paper is measured as if it were on it; the error grows with height and viewing angle.
  • No lens-distortion correction: barrel/pincushion distortion bends grid lines; error grows away from the calibration square and near the frame edges.

Tips for Accurate Results

  • Calibrate on a large square (e.g. a 10×10 block with P1–P4 set to (0,0), (10,0), (10,10), (0,10)).
  • Keep the object inside or near the calibration square.
  • Avoid wide-angle lenses and the edges of the frame; photograph as square-on as practical.
  • Zoom in when placing points; the higher the Scale (px/unit), the more precise each click.

Export & Append

  • Export CSV: downloads gridmeasure_YYYY-MM-DD_HHMMSS.csv with one row per calibrated image.
  • Append to CSV: pick an existing GridMeasure CSV; its rows and the current images are saved together as <name>_updated_YYYY-MM-DD_HHMMSS.csv. Columns are matched by name, so older exports merge correctly and extra columns you added are kept.
  • Only images whose calibration succeeded are exported. If any are skipped, you're asked to confirm first, with the skipped images listed.

What's in Each Row

  • image, image_width, image_height
  • grid_unit_size, grid_unit: the physical size of one grid unit (size is blank if not set)
  • P1_pixel_x/y … P4_pixel_x/y: calibration points in image pixels (2 decimals)
  • P1_grid_x/y … P4_grid_x/y: the grid coordinates they were mapped to
  • P5_grid_x/y, …: measurement points in grid units

Read columns by name, not by position, in downstream scripts.

Complete Reconstruction

📐
The four pixel/grid pairs fully determine the 3×3 homography H. Because H is invertible, downstream scripts can compute H-1 and recover the exact pixel position of every measurement point on the original image, so every result can be audited.
